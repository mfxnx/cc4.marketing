**Version:** 1.0
**Last Updated:** March 2026
**Owner:** Markit Agency

## Brand Personality Traits

Think of Planerio as a person. If Planerio walked into a room, they would be:

| Trait | What It Means in Practice |
|-------|--------------------------|
| **Confident** | Speaks with authority about remote work and productivity — no hedging, no vague claims |
| **Empathetic** | Genuinely understands the frustration of managing remote teams |
| **Straight-talking** | Gets to the point fast — no fluff, no filler |
| **Supportive** | Feels like a colleague helping you solve a problem, not a vendor pitching a product |
| **Optimistic** | Believes remote work can be done well — and that we can help |

### The "Smart Friend" Test
Write as if you're a smart friend who happens to be an expert in team productivity. Not a sales rep. Not a corporate newsletter. A real person who knows their stuff and wants to help.

## Tone of Voice

Planerio sits at a specific point on each of these scales:

### Formal ←————●————→ Casual
We lean **slightly casual**. Professional enough for a B2B audience, but never stiff or robotic. Think: a well-written Slack message from a respected colleague.

### Serious ←———●—————→ Playful
We lean **mostly serious**. Productivity and team management are real challenges — we take them seriously. Light wit is welcome when appropriate, but we never joke about our customers' pain points.

### Technical ←——————●———→ Simple
We lean **simple**. Our audience are managers, not engineers. Skip the jargon. Explain things clearly. If you need a technical term, define it.

### Reserved ←————●————→ Enthusiastic
We are **balanced**. Genuinely enthusiastic about what we do, but never over-the-top. Avoid excessive exclamation marks. Let the value speak for itself.

## Writing Style Guidelines

### Sentence Structure
- **Short sentences win.** Aim for under 20 words per sentence.
- **Vary your rhythm.** Short. Then a slightly longer one to add context. Then short again.
- **One idea per sentence.** Don't pile clauses on top of each other.

### Paragraphs
- Keep paragraphs to 2–3 sentences max in marketing copy
- Use white space generously — it's easier to read
- Lead with the most important point, then support it

### Voice
- Always use **active voice**: "Planerio protects your team's focus time" not "Focus time is protected by Planerio"
- Use **"you" and "we"** — speak directly to the reader
- **Benefits before features** — say what it does for them before explaining how it works

### Numbers and Specificity
- Use specific numbers when possible: "30% fewer meeting conflicts" not "fewer meetings"
- Spell out numbers one through nine; use numerals for 10 and above
- Always include the unit: "14-day free trial" not "free trial for 14 days"

## Do's and Don'ts

### Do's

| Do | Example |
|----|---------|
| Lead with the reader's problem | "Managing a remote team is hard. Coordinating focus time shouldn't be." |
| Use concrete benefits | "Your team gets 2 more hours of deep work per day." |
| Acknowledge real challenges | "We know your calendar is already a mess." |
| Be direct | "Try it free for 14 days." |
| Use conversational contractions | "You don't need another tool. You need the right one." |
| Show, don't tell | "Teams using Planerio report 30% fewer interruptions." |

### Don'ts

| Don't | Instead |
|-------|---------|
| Use buzzwords | ~~"Leverage our cutting-edge AI"~~ → "Use AI to protect focus time" |
| Over-promise | ~~"10x your team's productivity"~~ → "Help your team do their best work" |
| Be passive | ~~"Meetings are reduced by Planerio"~~ → "Planerio reduces meetings" |
| Use filler superlatives | ~~"Best-in-class, world-class, industry-leading"~~ → Just say what you do |
| Talk down to readers | ~~"You need to understand that remote work requires..."~~ → "Remote work is different. Here's what works." |
| Stack adjectives | ~~"Powerful, intuitive, seamless productivity platform"~~ → "A productivity tool built for remote teams" |

## Good vs Bad Copy Examples

### Website Headline

**Bad:**
> "The revolutionary AI-powered productivity platform that seamlessly synergizes your team's workflow to deliver best-in-class results."

**Good:**
> "Coordinate your team's focus time. Do more, interrupt less."

---

### Email Subject Line

**Bad:**
> "Leverage Planerio's cutting-edge features to maximize your team's productivity potential"

**Good:**
> "Your team lost 6 hours to bad meetings this week"

---

### Feature Description

**Bad:**
> "Our robust AI engine utilizes machine learning algorithms to optimize focus block scheduling across your distributed workforce."

**Good:**
> "Planerio's AI finds the best focus windows for your team — automatically. No more back-and-forth on Slack."

---

### Call to Action

**Bad:**
> "Click here to learn more about our product offerings"

**Good:**
> "Start your free 14-day trial — no credit card needed"

---

### LinkedIn Post

**Bad:**
> "Excited to share that our revolutionary platform is disrupting the productivity space! 🚀🚀🚀 Game-changing features incoming!!!"

**Good:**
> "Remote teams lose an average of 31 hours per month to unproductive meetings. That's almost a full work week. Planerio was built to fix that."

## Key Phrases and Terminology

### Use These Terms

| Term | Notes |
|------|-------|
| **Focus time** | Our core concept — protect it, coordinate it, measure it |
| **Deep work** | Acceptable alternative to focus time |
| **Coordinate** | Our primary verb — use it often |
| **Remote team** | Always "remote team", not "distributed workforce" or "virtual team" |
| **Protect** | Use when talking about guarding focus time from interruptions |
| **Visibility** | What managers get — clarity without micromanagement |
| **In flow / in sync** | Natural language for productive states |
| **Free trial** | Always "14-day free trial" — never just "free trial" |

### Avoid These Terms

| Avoid | Why |
|-------|-----|
| Seamless | Overused, meaningless |
| Leverage | Corporate jargon — just say "use" |
| Synergy | Never. Just never. |
| Revolutionary / Disruptive | Hyperbole — let the product speak |
| Workforce | Too corporate — say "team" |
| Utilize | Just say "use" |
| Solutions | Vague — say what you actually do |
| Robust | Says nothing — say what makes it powerful |
| Best-in-class | Unverifiable — use real proof instead |

## Channel-Specific Tone Adjustments

| Channel | Adjustment | Example |
|---------|-----------|---------|
| **Website** | Clear, benefit-focused, confident | "Coordinate your team's focus time." |
| **Email** | Conversational, helpful, a little warmer | "Hey — quick question about your team's schedule..." |
| **LinkedIn** | Professional, data-driven, thoughtful | "Remote teams lose 31 hours/month to unproductive meetings. Here's why." |
| **Twitter/X** | Punchy, direct, slightly informal | "Your team can't focus if your calendar won't let them." |
| **Ads** | Ultra-concise, benefit-first, clear CTA | "Less meetings. More output. Try free." |
| **In-app** | Friendly, encouraging, brief | "Nice work — your team has 3 focus blocks protected this week." |

```
VOICE:     Confident colleague, not corporate robot

PERSON:    You / We
STRUCTURE: Problem → Solution → Proof → CTA
AVOID:     Jargon, buzzwords, passive voice, hyperbole
REMEMBER:  Benefits before features. Always.
```
