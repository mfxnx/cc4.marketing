## Post 3: How to Build an Async-First Culture That Actually Works
**Target persona:** Founder Fiona
**Funnel stage:** Awareness / Consideration
**Target keyword:** async first remote team culture
**Word count:** 1,600
**Publish date:** May 18

### Outline

**Intro — The async myth**
- Most async-first attempts fail within 3 months
- Not because async doesn't work — because teams confuse async with "no system"
- What async-first actually requires: shared rhythm, not shared location

**Section 1: Why async fails without coordination**
- Everyone works at different times → no one knows when to collaborate
- Notifications fill the gaps → you're still always on
- Decisions slow down → async becomes an excuse for avoidance

**Section 2: The three ingredients of async-first culture**
1. **Communication norms** — when to use what (Slack vs. Loom vs. email vs. doc)
2. **Shared focus blocks** — times the whole team is heads-down, no interruptions
3. **Defined collaboration windows** — when meetings and feedback are welcome

**Section 3: How to roll it out in 4 weeks**
- Week 1: Document your communication norms — get team buy-in
- Week 2: Set team-wide focus blocks (start with 2 hours/day)
- Week 3: Move recurring meetings to collaboration windows
- Week 4: Review and adjust based on team feedback

**Section 4: Tools that support async rhythm**
- Notion for async docs and decisions
- Loom for async video updates
- Planerio for coordinating focus time team-wide
- Linear/Jira for task visibility without check-ins

**Section 5: Common mistakes and how to avoid them**
- No-meeting Fridays without protecting the rest of the week
- Too many async tools creating more noise than clarity
- Forgetting time zones when setting shared focus blocks

**Conclusion + CTA**
- "Async isn't a policy. It's a system. Build the system first."
- CTA: "Planerio helps remote teams build async rhythm from day one — try it free"
